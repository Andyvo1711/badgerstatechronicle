On Tuesday mornings at Evergreen Vitality Center in Appleton, it's not unusual to see a grandmother, her adult daughter, and a teenage grandson moving through the same balance and mobility circuit together. That intergenerational mix is by design. Founder Dr. Susan Kwan built the center's newest programming around the idea that healthy aging is rarely an individual project, even though most wellness marketing treats it that way.

"So much of the healthy aging industry talks to older adults in isolation, as though the people around them don't matter to the outcome," Kwan said. "In practice, the seniors who do best are usually the ones with family members involved in some way, whether that's showing up to a class with them or just reinforcing habits at home."

Kwan, a physician who spent years in geriatric care before opening Evergreen Vitality Center, designed the facility's newest offering, a multigenerational mobility and balance class, specifically to accommodate a wide range of physical ability levels within a single session.

## Programming Built Around Function, Not Aesthetics

Unlike much of the fitness industry's messaging around aging, which Kwan said still leans heavily on appearance-focused language, Evergreen's classes are structured around functional benchmarks: grip strength, single-leg balance duration, and the ability to rise from a seated position without using hands, all metrics with direct clinical relevance to fall risk and independence.

"We track things that actually predict whether someone can keep living independently, not whether they look a certain way," Kwan said. Instructors administer informal functional assessments every eight weeks, allowing clients to see measurable progress over time rather than relying on subjective impressions of improvement.

The center has also introduced a falls-prevention consultation service, pairing physical therapists with clients to assess home environments and recommend both exercise interventions and practical home modifications, an offering Kwan said has proven popular with adult children helping aging parents plan for continued independent living.

## A Growing Local Demand

Appleton's aging population has been growing steadily, part of a broader trend across the Fox Valley, and Kwan said demand for the center's senior-focused programming has outpaced her initial expectations since its expansion last year. Evergreen now runs twelve weekly classes specifically geared toward adults over sixty, up from four when Kwan first opened the current location.

Client Harold Beck, 76, who attends the multigenerational class with his granddaughter most weeks, said the shared structure changed his own consistency. "I skipped classes constantly when it was just me showing up alone," Beck said. "Now I don't want to be the reason she misses it, so I show up. It works both ways, honestly, because she says the same thing about me."

Kwan said she's now working with a local physical therapy clinic to formalize referral pathways for patients recovering from falls or hip surgery, aiming to position Evergreen as a maintenance step after formal rehabilitation ends rather than a replacement for it. "The gap between finishing physical therapy and actually staying strong long-term is where a lot of people fall through the cracks," Kwan said. "That's the specific space we're trying to fill."
