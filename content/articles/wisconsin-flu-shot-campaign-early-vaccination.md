Pharmacies and clinics across Wisconsin are ramping up flu vaccination outreach this fall, with public health officials urging residents to get their shots before Thanksgiving rather than waiting until symptoms start appearing in their communities. The push comes after last winter's flu season peaked later than usual, catching some hospital emergency departments in the state off guard with a wave of cases stretching into February.

Health systems including Aurora Health Care and Bellin Health have expanded weekend and evening vaccination clinics this year, part of a coordinated effort to make getting a flu shot as convenient as possible for residents balancing work schedules and family obligations.

## Lessons From Last Season

Emergency department volumes at several Wisconsin hospitals climbed sharply during last year's late-season flu surge, with some facilities reporting wait times that stretched well beyond typical levels for weeks at a stretch. A public health official who tracks respiratory illness trends statewide said the delayed peak caught some hospitals with lower staffing levels than they might have planned for a more typical, earlier season.

"Flu doesn't follow a fixed calendar," the official said. "When it arrives late, hospitals that had already scaled back their surge staffing can find themselves stretched thin right as case counts climb."

Officials are hoping that earlier vaccination this year, paired with clearer public messaging about the unpredictability of flu timing, will help blunt the impact of a similar late-season wave if one develops again.

## Vaccination Rates Vary Widely by County

State health data has shown persistent gaps in flu vaccination rates between Wisconsin's urban and rural counties, as well as across age groups. Adults between eighteen and forty-nine consistently show the lowest vaccination rates statewide, a pattern public health officials attribute partly to lower perceived risk among younger, generally healthy adults.

A nurse coordinating vaccination outreach in central Wisconsin said community clinics have tried to counter that perception by emphasizing that vaccination protects not just the individual but also more vulnerable family members, including infants too young to be vaccinated themselves and older relatives at higher risk of complications.

"We ask people to think beyond just themselves," the nurse said. "A mild case in a healthy adult can turn into something much more serious if it spreads to a grandparent or a newborn in the same household."

## Combination Visits and Pharmacy Access

Many Wisconsin clinics are now offering flu shots alongside routine appointments, aiming to reduce the number of separate trips patients need to make. Pharmacies have also expanded walk-in vaccination hours, a convenience that public health officials credit with reaching patients who might otherwise skip the shot entirely due to scheduling friction.

Insurance coverage for flu vaccination remains broad across Wisconsin, with most plans covering the shot at no out-of-pocket cost, and community clinics offering reduced-cost or free vaccination for uninsured residents through state-supported programs.

## A Simple Message

Despite the operational complexity behind this year's vaccination push, the message from health officials remains straightforward: get vaccinated before flu activity picks up, rather than after. Historical data shows it typically takes about two weeks for full immunity to develop after vaccination, a window health officials say residents should factor into their planning as holiday gatherings and colder weather approach.

Whether this year's early outreach translates into higher vaccination rates and a less severe season won't be clear until winter arrives in earnest, but hospital administrators say they would rather be over-prepared than caught flat-footed a second year in a row.
