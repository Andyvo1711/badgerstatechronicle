Construction crews broke ground this week on a new residence hall near the University of Wisconsin–Milwaukee campus, part of an effort administrators say is meant to relieve years of tight housing supply that has pushed many students into an increasingly expensive off-campus rental market. The project is expected to add several hundred beds when it opens, with a mix of traditional dormitory-style rooms and suite-style units aimed at upperclassmen.

University housing officials say demand has outpaced supply for much of the past decade, particularly as UW-Milwaukee has worked to boost retention by encouraging students to stay on or near campus through their sophomore and junior years, rather than scattering into the surrounding neighborhoods after their first year.

## A Response to a Tightening Market

"We've had years where students were waitlisted for on-campus housing well into the summer," said a UW-Milwaukee housing services representative. "That's not a good position to put students in right before the semester starts, especially students who are already juggling work and family responsibilities."

The surrounding neighborhoods near the campus, including parts of the East Side and Riverwest, have also seen rental prices climb in recent years, driven in part by broader housing pressures across the Milwaukee metro area. University officials say the new hall is intended to give students a more predictable and often more affordable alternative to competing in that market, particularly for those who don't have a car or reliable transportation to campus.

The new building will include shared kitchen spaces, study lounges, and a small number of accessible units designed for students with mobility needs. Sustainability features, including energy-efficient heating systems, are also part of the design, reflecting broader campus commitments to reducing energy costs over the building's lifespan.

## Balancing Cost and Capacity

Funding for the project comes through a combination of university housing revenue bonds and state capital funds, with room and board rates expected to be set closer to the building's completion. Student government representatives have pushed administrators to keep costs comparable to existing dorms, expressing concern that a shiny new building could otherwise become an option only wealthier students can afford.

"We want more beds, but we don't want this to just become the expensive option that prices out the students who need it most," said a student government representative involved in discussions with university housing staff.

University officials say they are aware of that tension and have committed to pricing the new hall within a similar range to existing university housing, though final numbers will depend on construction costs, which have fluctuated with material and labor prices in recent years.

## What's Next

The residence hall is expected to take roughly two years to complete, with an anticipated opening for the 2028 academic year. In the meantime, university officials say they are exploring interim measures, including partnerships with nearby apartment complexes to guarantee blocks of units for students at negotiated rates.

For a campus that has long balanced its identity as both a research university and a commuter-friendly option for Milwaukee-area students, the new housing project marks a notable investment in encouraging more students to live, study, and build community directly on campus.
