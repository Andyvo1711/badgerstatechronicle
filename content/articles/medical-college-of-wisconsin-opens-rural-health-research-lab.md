The Medical College of Wisconsin has opened a new research lab dedicated to studying health outcomes in the state's rural communities, an initiative researchers say addresses a long-standing gap in medical research that has historically concentrated on urban populations. The lab will focus on issues ranging from maternal health access in counties without a local obstetrics unit to chronic disease management in areas with limited specialist care.

Wisconsin's rural counties, particularly in the northern and western parts of the state, have seen a steady decline in local hospital services over the past two decades, with several small hospitals closing labor and delivery units or reducing emergency room hours due to staffing and financial pressures. Researchers say the new lab will work to quantify the health effects of those changes and identify interventions that could help.

## Filling a Data Gap

"A lot of national health research just doesn't capture what's happening in a county with one clinic and a forty-five minute drive to the nearest hospital," said a researcher affiliated with the new lab. "We need Wisconsin-specific data if we want Wisconsin-specific solutions."

The lab's initial projects include a multi-year study tracking pregnancy outcomes in counties that have lost local birthing units, as well as research into telehealth adoption rates among older rural residents managing chronic conditions like diabetes and heart disease. Researchers say early telehealth data suggests uneven results, with some patients benefiting significantly from remote monitoring while others face barriers due to limited broadband access or unfamiliarity with the technology.

## Training the Next Generation of Rural Physicians

Beyond pure research, the lab is also expected to serve as a training ground for medical students and residents interested in rural practice. The Medical College of Wisconsin has for years operated programs aimed at encouraging students to consider rural placements after graduation, part of a broader effort to address physician shortages that afflict much of rural Wisconsin more severely than the state's metro areas.

"Exposure matters a lot here," said a program administrator connected to the college's rural health initiatives. "Students who spend meaningful time doing research or clinical rotations in rural communities are more likely to consider practicing there long-term. That's really the end goal of a lot of this work."

## Community Partnerships

The lab is coordinating with several rural hospital systems and community health centers across the state, which will provide de-identified patient data and help connect researchers with local health officials. Officials from participating rural health systems say they welcome the added research attention, noting that rural hospitals often lack the staff and resources to conduct this kind of research themselves, even though they sit closest to the populations being studied.

"We see the problems every day, but we don't always have the bandwidth to study them formally," said an administrator at a rural hospital system partnering with the new lab. "Having a research partner who can dig into the data and help us make the case for resources is genuinely valuable."

## Long-Term Goals

Researchers involved with the lab say they hope findings will eventually inform state health policy discussions, including debates over rural hospital funding and telehealth reimbursement rules. While the lab's work is still in its early stages, faculty say they expect to publish initial findings from the pregnancy outcomes study within the next two to three years, with additional projects added as funding allows.
