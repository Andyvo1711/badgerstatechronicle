Construction activity across southeastern Wisconsin has picked up noticeably this spring, with contractors reporting fuller project pipelines spanning commercial development, infrastructure work, and industrial facility expansion. The uptick spans several counties surrounding Milwaukee and Racine, areas that have benefited in recent years from a mix of manufacturing investment and public infrastructure spending.

General contractors and construction trade associations describe a market that feels notably busier than the more uncertain conditions of the past couple of years, when elevated interest rates and material cost volatility caused some developers to delay or scale back planned projects. That caution appears to be easing somewhat as financing conditions stabilize and material costs, while still elevated compared with pre-pandemic levels, have become more predictable.

## Industrial and Manufacturing Facilities Lead Demand

Much of the current construction momentum traces back to industrial and manufacturing facility projects, including new production space, warehouse expansions, and equipment upgrades tied to Wisconsin's broader manufacturing sector. Contractors specializing in industrial construction describe strong demand from both existing manufacturers expanding capacity and new facilities associated with supply chain investments tied to electric vehicle components, battery technology, and advanced manufacturing more broadly.

Commercial developers point to renewed confidence among manufacturers as a key driver, noting that companies generally do not commit to major facility expansions unless they have reasonable confidence in sustained demand for their products over the multi-year timeline a new facility represents.

## Skilled Labor Shortages Persist as a Bottleneck

Even as project pipelines fill, contractors across the region continue to cite skilled trades labor availability as their most significant operational constraint. Electricians, plumbers, and heavy equipment operators remain in particularly short supply, a shortage that has pushed project timelines longer in some cases and contributed to continued wage growth within the construction trades.

"We could take on more work right now if we had the crews to staff it, that's really the honest answer," said a general contractor based in Racine County, describing a hiring environment where finding experienced tradespeople has become the primary limiting factor on how much work the firm can accept. Trade unions and contractor associations have expanded apprenticeship recruitment efforts in response, targeting high school and technical college students with messaging aimed at countering a long-standing perception gap between trades careers and four-year college paths.

## Material Costs Stabilize but Remain Elevated

Construction material costs, which spiked sharply in prior years, have generally stabilized at levels still above pre-pandemic norms. Lumber, steel, and concrete prices have shown less volatility recently than in past cycles, giving contractors more confidence in bidding fixed-price contracts without the extensive cost escalation clauses that became standard practice during periods of rapid price swings.

Some contractors note that certain specialty materials and equipment, particularly items tied to electrical infrastructure and HVAC systems, continue to face longer lead times than historical norms, requiring more advanced project planning and procurement scheduling than contractors were accustomed to in prior years.

## Public Infrastructure Spending Adds Momentum

Beyond private commercial and industrial projects, public infrastructure spending tied to road, bridge, and utility improvements continues to support construction activity across the region. State and federal infrastructure funding has provided a steady stream of public works projects that contractors describe as a valuable complement to more cyclical private commercial development, offering a degree of workload stability that helps firms retain skilled crews between larger private projects.

Looking ahead, contractors and industry economists describe cautious optimism about continued construction growth through the remainder of the year, provided interest rates remain relatively stable and manufacturing investment, the sector currently driving much of the region's industrial construction demand, continues on its current trajectory.
