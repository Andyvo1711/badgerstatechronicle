Theodore Lindqvist installed his first residential solar array in 2012 on a farmhouse roof outside La Crosse, working alongside a single employee and a borrowed pickup truck, in a region where clean energy contractors were scarce and skepticism about solar's viability in Wisconsin's cloudy winters ran high. More than a decade later, Lindqvist runs Bluff Country Solar, a company with more than 50 employees that has become one of the largest solar installation and clean energy consulting firms in western Wisconsin.

Lindqvist's interest in renewable energy began during an environmental science degree, followed by several years working for a Minneapolis-based solar installer before he decided the La Crosse region, with its mix of agricultural land, small manufacturers, and a receptive local government, was underserved by the clean energy industry. He returned home in 2011 and spent a year building relationships with local electricians and contractors before formally launching Bluff Country Solar.

## Overcoming Regional Skepticism

Early on, Lindqvist faced persistent doubts from potential customers convinced that Wisconsin's climate made solar an impractical investment, a misconception he has spent much of his career correcting through public data and demonstration projects. He partnered with a local credit union to finance a visible rooftop array atop its main branch, using the building's public energy production data to show skeptical residents and business owners that solar generation remained viable even through the region's snowier months.

That strategy of visible, data-backed demonstration projects became central to Bluff Country Solar's growth. The company has since installed systems on dozens of area farms, several manufacturing facilities, and a growing number of municipal buildings, including a solar array atop a La Crosse public works facility that Lindqvist has said became one of his proudest projects, both for its visibility and for the public sector relationships it opened.

## Diversifying Beyond Rooftop Installation

As the residential and commercial solar market has grown more competitive, with national installers entering the region, Lindqvist has diversified Bluff Country Solar's business into energy storage system installation and clean energy consulting for municipalities and school districts evaluating renewable energy investments. He has said this consulting arm, while smaller than the installation business, has become an important buffer against pricing pressure from larger out-of-state competitors.

## Building a Regional Workforce

Wisconsin's solar installation labor market remains thin outside major metro areas, and Lindqvist has invested significantly in training, partnering with a local technical college to help develop a renewable energy technician certificate program that did not previously exist in the La Crosse area. Several of the program's early graduates now work directly for Bluff Country Solar, and Lindqvist has said building this local talent pipeline was as important to the company's long-term viability as any single contract.

## Navigating Policy Uncertainty

Like solar companies nationally, Bluff Country Solar has had to adapt to shifting state and federal incentive structures, and Lindqvist has become an active voice in state renewable energy policy discussions, testifying before legislative committees on net metering rules that affect how solar customers are compensated for excess electricity generation. He has argued that policy stability, more than any single incentive program, is what allows companies like his to plan multi-year hiring and investment decisions with confidence.

"People still ask me if solar really works here," Lindqvist said, gesturing toward the bluffs outside his office window. "The data's been answering that question for years. We just have to keep showing it to people."
