Aurora Health Care is adding operating room capacity and surgical staff dedicated to orthopedic procedures across several of its southeastern Wisconsin hospitals, responding to rising demand for joint replacement and sports medicine surgeries that health system leaders say has outpaced their current scheduling capacity.

The expansion includes additional dedicated orthopedic operating suites, an increase in surgical staffing, and a new same-day joint replacement pathway for eligible patients, allowing certain hip and knee replacement candidates to return home the day of surgery rather than staying overnight, a model that has become increasingly common at high-volume orthopedic centers nationally.

## An Aging, Active Population Drives Demand

Wisconsin's demographic trends are a significant factor behind the growing demand for orthopedic procedures, as a large cohort of residents moves through the age range when joint wear and degenerative conditions like osteoarthritis become more common. At the same time, health system leaders note that older adults today often remain more physically active than previous generations, driving demand for procedures that restore mobility and allow patients to continue activities like golfing, hiking, or simply keeping up with grandchildren.

An orthopedic surgery department leader at Aurora said scheduling backlogs had grown noticeably over the past two years. "We were seeing patients wait several months for a joint replacement consultation, and that's frustrating when someone is dealing with daily pain that limits basic activities," the department leader said. "This expansion is a direct response to that gap."

## Sports Medicine Growth Among Younger Patients

The orthopedic expansion isn't limited to joint replacement. Aurora's sports medicine program has also seen rising volume, driven in part by youth and high school athletics across southeastern Wisconsin, where injuries like torn ACLs and rotator cuff damage have become more common as competitive youth sports participation has intensified.

A sports medicine surgeon within the Aurora network said earlier, more specialized diagnosis has become important as young athletes and their families push for faster returns to competition. "Parents and coaches want a clear timeline, and athletes want to get back to their sport safely," the surgeon said. "Having dedicated capacity lets us move more quickly from diagnosis to treatment plan without adding to a backlog shared with older patients needing joint replacements."

## Same-Day Surgery Pathways

The introduction of a same-day discharge pathway for eligible joint replacement patients reflects broader shifts in orthopedic surgery nationally, supported by improved pain management protocols, minimally invasive surgical techniques, and more structured pre-surgical patient education. Patients selected for the pathway typically meet specific health criteria and complete education sessions in advance covering what to expect during recovery at home.

A physical therapist who works with same-day surgery patients said preparation is critical to the model's success. "Patients need to walk in already knowing their home setup, their pain management plan, and who's going to help them during the first forty-eight hours," she said. "When that groundwork is done ahead of time, going home the same day is often safer and more comfortable than people expect."

## Staffing the Expansion

Growing surgical capacity requires more than additional operating rooms. Aurora has also been recruiting orthopedic surgeons, physician assistants, and specialized surgical nurses to support the expanded schedule, competing for talent in a specialty that remains in high demand nationally.

Hospital officials say they expect the expanded capacity to meaningfully reduce wait times for both joint replacement consultations and sports medicine evaluations over the coming year, though they acknowledge that continued growth in demand may require further investment down the road.
