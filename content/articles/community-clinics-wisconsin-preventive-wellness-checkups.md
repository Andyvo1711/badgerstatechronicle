As the new year begins, community health clinics across Wisconsin are launching renewed outreach campaigns encouraging residents to schedule annual wellness checkups, part of a statewide effort to catch chronic conditions like diabetes and high cholesterol before they progress into more serious, costlier health problems.

The push includes reminder calls and texts to patients who haven't had a checkup in over a year, expanded weekend clinic hours, and, in several communities, partnerships with local employers to offer on-site screening events during the workday. Clinic leaders describe the timing as intentional, capitalizing on the natural motivation many people feel at the start of a new year to address health goals they may have been putting off.

## The Cost of Deferred Care

Public health data has consistently shown that patients who skip routine preventive visits are more likely to be diagnosed with chronic conditions at a later, more advanced stage, when treatment tends to be more complex and expensive. A community clinic director in central Wisconsin said the pattern shows up clearly in her clinic's own patient data.

"We see patients who haven't had a checkup in three or four years come in with blood sugar levels or blood pressure readings that clearly didn't develop overnight," the director said. "If we'd caught it a year or two earlier, we'd likely be talking about lifestyle changes instead of starting medication immediately."

Annual wellness visits typically include basic screenings for blood pressure, cholesterol, and blood sugar, along with age-appropriate cancer screening referrals and a review of vaccination status. For many patients, especially those without chronic conditions requiring more frequent monitoring, the annual visit represents their only regular point of contact with the healthcare system.

## Removing Barriers to Scheduling

Community clinics have also worked to reduce practical barriers that keep patients from scheduling preventive visits in the first place. Expanded evening and Saturday hours have been a common addition, aimed at patients who work daytime shifts and previously had to take time off work for a routine appointment.

A patient navigator at a community clinic in the Fox Valley said transportation and cost concerns are frequently cited as reasons patients delay care, even when a visit would be fully covered by insurance or available on a sliding fee scale. "A lot of people don't realize what's actually covered until we walk them through it," she said. "Once that barrier is addressed, scheduling the visit becomes much less daunting."

## Employer Partnerships Extend Reach

Several clinics have begun partnering directly with local employers to bring basic screenings on-site during work hours, an approach that has proven particularly effective for reaching adults who might otherwise deprioritize their own healthcare needs amid busy schedules. Employees can complete a blood pressure check and basic health questionnaire during a break, with clinic staff following up directly if results suggest a need for a full appointment.

## Measuring Long-Term Impact

Clinic administrators acknowledge that the full impact of increased preventive care outreach takes years to become fully visible in population-level health data, since the benefit lies in conditions caught earlier and complications avoided down the road rather than an immediate, measurable outcome. Still, several community health centers report modest increases in annual checkup completion rates since expanding their outreach efforts.

For clinic staff working the phones and coordinating outreach texts each January, the goal remains simple: get more Wisconsin residents through the door for a routine checkup, before a manageable issue has the chance to become a serious one.
