For years, Milwaukee's East Side was known primarily as a walkable residential neighborhood popular with young professionals and university students, its identity built around coffee shops, independent bookstores, and proximity to the lakefront. Increasingly, it's also becoming known for something else: a dense concentration of spas, yoga studios, and wellness-focused businesses drawing overnight visitors from Madison, the Fox Valley, and beyond.

Talia Reyes, owner of Northpoint Wellness Retreat, a boutique property near Lake Park that combines short-stay guest suites with an in-house spa, has become one of the more visible advocates for marketing the neighborhood as a coordinated wellness destination rather than a collection of unrelated businesses.

"Individually, none of us are big enough to be a destination on our own," Reyes said. "But if someone can book a weekend package that includes a facial at one studio, a yoga class at another, and a farm-to-table dinner within walking distance, that's a genuinely compelling reason to drive in from two hours away."

## A Loose Coalition of Local Businesses

Over the past year, Reyes has worked informally with roughly a dozen East Side businesses, including massage studios, a float therapy center, and two yoga studios, to cross-promote packages and coordinate scheduling for visitors booking multi-service weekends. There's no formal business association behind the effort yet, just a shared group chat and a willingness among owners to refer clients to one another rather than compete for the same limited local customer base.

"We realized pretty quickly that a visitor spending money at my studio and then walking two blocks to spend money at a spa is better for both of us than either of us trying to keep that whole spend in-house," said Yuki Tanaka, owner of a nearby float therapy studio that has become a regular referral partner for Northpoint.

Reyes said data from her own guest bookings shows a clear pattern: roughly sixty percent of Northpoint's overnight guests over the past year have come from outside Milwaukee County, with a noticeable share arriving from Madison and Appleton for two-night wellness-focused weekends.

## Infrastructure Still Catching Up

The neighborhood's wellness tourism growth has outpaced some of its supporting infrastructure. Reyes said parking remains a persistent complaint among visiting guests, and public transit options connecting the East Side to downtown hotels and the airport remain limited compared to what a more established wellness tourism destination might offer.

There's also the matter of formal recognition. Reyes has begun conversations with Milwaukee's tourism promotion organization about including East Side wellness businesses in broader visitor marketing campaigns, arguing that the current visitor guides underrepresent the neighborhood compared to more traditional attractions downtown and near the lakefront.

"We're not asking to replace anything," Reyes said. "We just think a wellness weekend on the East Side deserves to be listed alongside a Brewers game or a trip to the art museum as a reason to visit Milwaukee."

Guest Priya Deshmukh, who traveled from Madison for a wellness weekend at Northpoint with two friends, said the draw was less about any single treatment and more about the cumulative experience. "You don't get that in Madison, at least not in one walkable area," Deshmukh said. "We did a spa morning, a yoga class in the afternoon, and dinner without ever needing a car. That's rare."

Reyes said she expects the informal coalition to formalize further this year, with early discussions underway about a shared marketing budget and a jointly produced visitor guide.
