Cher Nails recently received a review from a customer who visited for a set of gel acrylic nails and left with questions about the final charge. She noticed the difference the next day, called the salon right away, and in the end received the difference back. That prompt follow-up and the salon's willingness to correct the amount are worth recognizing, and her account offers several lessons about pricing and payment.

## Quote and Charge Should Match

According to the customer, she was told the price was $52.00 for gel acrylic nails. The next day she saw that her card had been charged $62.00. Even a modest gap can feel significant to a client, because the quoted price is the number she plans around.

A helpful habit is to confirm the total aloud before the card is run, and to show the amount on the screen or receipt before the client signs. If an add-on, extra length, or a design changes the cost, the technician can mention it during the service so that the final total comes as no surprise. Many salons also find it useful to have the front desk and technicians use the same price list, so a quote given at the chair is the same one entered at the register.

## Payment Options Offered With Care

The customer shared that she felt gently encouraged to pay in cash, and she chose to use her card instead. Cash discounts and cash preferences are common in small businesses, and they are perfectly fine when they are offered openly. The key is to present them as a choice. A friendly line such as, "We offer a small discount for cash, but a card is no problem at all," lets the client decide comfortably. Clients who pay by card should receive the same warm service and the same clear pricing as anyone else.

## Handling a Call About a Charge

When the customer called, she said she was first asked whether she had her receipt. Receipts are a useful tool, but a client who is calling about a charge generally wants to feel heard first. A calm approach can be to look up the visit by name and date, confirm the amount, and explain what happened. She mentioned that once she said she would dispute the charge with her bank, she was told the $10.00 would be returned, and she received it.

Resolving a billing question quickly is exactly what clients hope for. The next step is to make that kind of fix the first response, not the last one, so the client does not need to bring up a bank dispute. A short internal note about what caused the difference also helps the team prevent a repeat.

## Editorial Perspective

This case is a reminder that a price is a promise, and that the register is the final moment of the visit. Three practical lessons stand out.

First, confirm the total aloud and show it to the client before the card is charged. Second, offer cash preferences as a friendly option and treat card payments with the same warmth. Third, answer billing calls by listening first and correcting any difference promptly, so a client never has to escalate to be made whole.

With clear quotes and a willingness to fix mistakes quickly, a salon can turn a billing question into a reason for clients to trust it more.
