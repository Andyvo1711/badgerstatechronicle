Monique Beauregard opened the first Prairie & Pine Mercantile storefront on Oshkosh's Main Street in 2014 with roughly 400 square feet of retail space, a handful of consignment furniture pieces, and a firm belief that shoppers still wanted a curated, tactile alternative to big-box stores and online marketplaces. Twelve years later, she operates four Prairie & Pine Mercantile locations across northeastern Wisconsin, employing more than 60 people and anchoring what local business groups have credited as part of a broader revival of downtown Oshkosh's retail corridor.

Beauregard's retail career began not in Oshkosh but in Minneapolis, where she managed visual merchandising for a regional home goods chain before growing disillusioned with what she described as an increasingly homogenized retail landscape. She returned to her hometown of Oshkosh in 2013 with savings from nearly a decade in retail management and a plan to build a store that combined locally made goods, vintage furniture, and small-batch home products, betting that shoppers fatigued by chain stores would respond to a more personal retail experience.

## Betting on Downtown When Others Were Leaving

Beauregard opened her first store during a period when several long-standing downtown Oshkosh retailers were closing, unable to compete with a nearby shopping mall and growing e-commerce competition. She has said that timing worked in her favor, as falling commercial rents made a downtown storefront financially feasible for a first-time retailer with limited startup capital. "Everyone told me downtown retail was dying," Beauregard said in a recent interview at her flagship store. "I thought it just needed something people couldn't get anywhere else."

That philosophy shaped Prairie & Pine Mercantile's buying strategy from the beginning. Rather than stocking mass-produced inventory, Beauregard built relationships with dozens of Wisconsin artisans, furniture makers, and small food producers, positioning the store as a showcase for regional craftsmanship as much as a retail outlet. The approach proved popular enough that she opened a second location in Appleton in 2018, followed by stores in Fond du Lac and a second Oshkosh location near the University of Wisconsin-Oshkosh campus.

## Balancing Growth With Curation

Expanding to multiple locations introduced challenges Beauregard had not faced as a single-store owner, particularly maintaining the carefully curated product mix that distinguished Prairie & Pine Mercantile from typical retail chains. She built a small internal buying team tasked with sourcing regionally appropriate inventory for each location rather than simply replicating the flagship store's offerings, a more labor-intensive approach than standard retail chain practice but one she considers essential to the brand's identity.

## Supporting Oshkosh's Small Business Ecosystem

Beauregard has become an active voice in downtown Oshkosh's business association, helping organize a recurring small business market event that brings dozens of local vendors to Main Street each summer. She has also mentored several first-time retail entrepreneurs informally, sharing lessons from her own early struggles securing inventory financing and negotiating commercial leases.

## Facing Online Competition Head-On

Rather than avoiding e-commerce, Beauregard built an online store for Prairie & Pine Mercantile that now accounts for a meaningful share of revenue, arguing that local retailers who ignore online sales are ceding ground unnecessarily. She has resisted, however, pressure from some advisors to expand into a purely online model, insisting that the in-person retail experience remains central to the brand. "People can buy things anywhere," she said. "They can't touch, smell, and talk to somebody about where it came from anywhere. That's still worth something."
