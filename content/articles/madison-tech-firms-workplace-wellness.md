Every other Thursday, a folding massage chair appears in a conference room at a mid-sized software company near Madison's East Washington Avenue corridor, and a line quietly forms outside. It's one of a growing number of Madison workplaces contracting with Capitol Corporate Wellness, a service that brings chair massage, guided meditation sessions, and occasional nutrition workshops directly into office buildings.

Founder Jordan Fiskum started the business four years ago after working in human resources at a Madison tech company and noticing how frequently burnout, rather than compensation, came up as a reason employees cited for leaving. "Nobody was asking for a ping pong table," Fiskum said. "They were asking for actual recovery time built into their week."

Capitol Corporate Wellness now contracts with fourteen employers across the Madison area, mostly technology and insurance companies concentrated along the East Washington corridor and near the Capitol Square, though Fiskum said interest has recently expanded into manufacturing and health care administration offices as well.

## Addressing Burnout Without Raising Pay

Fiskum is candid about the limits of what on-site wellness services can accomplish. "I'm not going to pretend fifteen minutes of chair massage fixes a genuinely toxic workplace or an unsustainable workload," Fiskum said. "What it can do is give people a real physical reset in the middle of a stressful week, and it signals that a company is willing to invest something tangible, not just talk about wellness in an email."

That distinction matters to employees like software engineer Malik Chen, who uses the biweekly massage sessions at his Madison employer. "It's not going to solve deadline pressure," Chen said. "But having fifteen minutes where nobody can message me and someone's actually working out the knot in my shoulder, that's real. I look forward to it more than I probably should."

Employers, meanwhile, are often motivated by retention data as much as employee wellbeing in the abstract. Fiskum said several client companies have cited reduced turnover in exit interview data as a factor in renewing their contracts, though she acknowledges isolating wellness programming as the specific cause is difficult given how many variables affect retention.

## Building a Team to Meet Demand

Meeting growing demand has required Capitol Corporate Wellness to expand its practitioner roster from two massage therapists to seven, along with two meditation instructors who rotate between client sites. Fiskum said finding practitioners comfortable working in a corporate office environment, rather than a traditional spa setting, has been a bigger hiring challenge than expected.

"Giving a great chair massage in a quiet spa room and giving one in a busy open-plan office with people walking by is genuinely a different skill," Fiskum said. "Some excellent therapists just don't want to work that way, and that's fair."

Fiskum said she's begun fielding inquiries from employers outside the Madison area, including a manufacturing company in Fitchburg and a health system administrative office in Sun Prairie, suggesting the workplace wellness model may be poised to expand beyond its initial downtown Madison footprint.

"Five years ago, I would have said this was a nice-to-have," Fiskum said. "I think a lot of employers now see it as closer to table stakes, especially in industries competing hard for talent."
