Beloit College, the small liberal arts institution near the Wisconsin-Illinois border, reported a notable uptick in its incoming class size this year, a welcome development for a school that, like many small private colleges nationally, has spent much of the past decade navigating demographic and financial headwinds. College officials attribute the growth to a combination of curriculum changes and a more aggressive recruiting strategy focused on students who might not have previously considered a small liberal arts education.

Enrollment challenges at small liberal arts colleges have been well documented across the country, driven by a shrinking pool of college-age students in much of the Midwest, rising skepticism about the value of a liberal arts degree relative to its cost, and stiff competition from larger public universities offering lower price tags. Beloit has not been immune to those pressures, and officials say the recent growth follows several years of deliberate strategic adjustments.

## Curriculum Redesign

Central to the college's pitch to prospective students has been a redesigned curriculum that emphasizes applied, project-based learning alongside traditional liberal arts coursework. The college has leaned into programs combining fields like environmental studies with data analysis, or literature with digital media production, aiming to counter the perception that a liberal arts degree lacks practical career application.

"We kept hearing the same objection from prospective families: 'this sounds great, but what job does it lead to,'" said a Beloit College admissions officer. "So we've worked to build clearer through-lines between what students study here and the kinds of roles they go on to fill, without giving up what makes a liberal arts education valuable in the first place."

## Recruiting Beyond Traditional Feeder Regions

The college has also expanded recruiting efforts into new geographic markets, including partnerships with community organizations in cities outside its traditional recruiting footprint, aiming to reach first-generation and lower-income students who might not otherwise consider a small private college. Financial aid packages have been restructured to be more transparent earlier in the admissions process, addressing a common complaint that students often don't learn their true cost of attendance until after they've already invested significant effort in an application.

"A lot of talented students self-select out of applying because they assume it's unaffordable," said the admissions officer. "Being upfront about aid earlier in the process has made a real difference in who ends up applying and enrolling."

## A Cautiously Optimistic Outlook

College leaders are careful not to overstate the significance of a single year's enrollment increase, noting that small colleges can see meaningful swings from class to class based on factors outside their control. Still, faculty and staff describe a shift in campus mood after several years of budget tightening and, in some departments, reduced course offerings.

"One good year doesn't undo a decade of pressure, but it does give us some breathing room and, honestly, some morale," said a longtime member of the college's faculty senate.

## Looking Ahead

Beloit officials say they plan to continue refining the curriculum changes based on outcomes data from current students, while expanding the recruiting partnerships that contributed to this year's growth. Whether the increase represents a durable trend or a one-year bump remains to be seen, but for now, the college is treating it as validation of a multi-year strategy to make the case for liberal arts education in a skeptical market.
