The University of Wisconsin–Eau Claire has opened a new academic building dedicated to undergraduate research, adding lab space, collaborative work areas, and presentation facilities intended to support a program the university has long promoted as a defining feature of its undergraduate experience. UW-Eau Claire has for years pointed to its undergraduate research participation rates as a differentiator from larger research universities, where such opportunities are often reserved primarily for graduate students.

The new building consolidates research space that had previously been scattered across several older academic buildings, some of which were not originally designed to accommodate modern lab equipment or the growing number of students participating in faculty-mentored research projects. University officials say the consolidation should make it easier to coordinate cross-disciplinary projects, since research groups from different departments will now share common building space rather than being spread across campus.

## A Long-Standing Institutional Priority

"Undergraduate research has been part of this university's identity for a long time, but our facilities hadn't necessarily kept pace with how much the program had grown," said a UW-Eau Claire administrator involved in planning the new building. "This is really about making sure our infrastructure matches our ambitions."

The building includes dedicated space for a range of disciplines, from wet labs used by biology and chemistry students to data analysis rooms equipped for social science and business research projects. A central atrium is designed to host the university's annual student research symposium, an event that previously required renting outside space to accommodate the growing number of student presenters.

## Faculty Mentorship at the Center

University faculty say the expanded research program depends heavily on close mentorship relationships between students and professors, a structure the new building's design was meant to reinforce through shared offices and adjacent lab space that puts faculty and student researchers in regular proximity.

"A lot of what makes undergraduate research valuable isn't just the lab work itself, it's the ongoing conversation with a faculty mentor about what the data means and what to try next," said a biology professor who has supervised student research projects for over a decade. "Having space that actually supports that kind of regular interaction matters more than people might expect."

## Student Experiences

Students involved in ongoing research projects said the new space has already made a practical difference, particularly for projects requiring specialized equipment that previously had to be shared across departments in ways that created scheduling conflicts. "Before, we were sometimes waiting weeks to get lab time because three other research groups needed the same equipment," said a senior chemistry student conducting research on water quality in local streams. "Now there's dedicated space for our project specifically."

## Funding and Broader Context

The building was funded through a mix of state capital investment and a fundraising campaign that drew notable support from alumni who credited their own undergraduate research experience with shaping their post-graduation careers, including several who went on to graduate programs at larger research institutions.

University leaders say the investment reflects a broader strategy among Wisconsin's regional public universities to compete for students not by matching the scale of flagship research institutions, but by offering more personalized access to research and mentorship than students might find at a larger school. With the new building now open, UW-Eau Claire officials say they expect undergraduate research participation to continue growing in the coming years.
