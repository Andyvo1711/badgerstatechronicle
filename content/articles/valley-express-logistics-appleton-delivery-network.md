Fiona Bregman founded Valley Express Logistics in 2013 after a frustrating stretch running operations for a small Fox Valley manufacturer, where she repeatedly watched national parcel carriers fail to offer reliable same-day or next-day delivery options for business-to-business shipments within the region. Rather than continuing to work around the problem, she decided to build a solution, starting with two delivery vans and a handful of manufacturing clients willing to give a new, unproven logistics company a chance.

Bregman's core insight, one she has repeated in nearly every interview and speaking engagement since, was that national carriers like the large parcel companies were optimized for long-haul, high-volume shipping networks, not the dense, regional delivery routes that many Fox Valley manufacturers and distributors actually needed. A part supplier in Appleton shipping to a customer in Oshkosh or Neenah often waited a full day or more for a package that, by delivery van, could arrive within hours. Valley Express Logistics was built specifically to fill that regional gap.

## Building Density Before Building Distance

Rather than trying to expand geographically as quickly as possible, Bregman focused the company's first several years on building delivery density within the Fox Valley corridor, adding routes between Appleton, Neenah, Oshkosh, and Green Bay before expanding further outward. This density-first strategy let Valley Express Logistics offer same-day delivery windows that larger carriers structurally could not match in the region, becoming a key selling point for manufacturers needing urgent parts deliveries and for growing e-commerce businesses needing reliable local fulfillment.

The company's client base has since expanded well beyond manufacturing to include regional e-commerce retailers, medical supply distributors, and a growing contract with several Fox Valley hospital systems requiring time-sensitive delivery of medical equipment and lab samples between facilities. Bregman has said the medical logistics contracts, while operationally demanding given strict chain-of-custody and timing requirements, have become one of the company's most stable and fastest-growing revenue lines.

## Navigating Fuel Costs and Driver Retention

Like most logistics companies, Valley Express Logistics has had to manage volatile fuel costs and a competitive labor market for delivery drivers, particularly as e-commerce giants have driven up regional wages for similar driving roles. Bregman responded by building one of the more generous driver benefits packages among regional logistics companies, including a profit-sharing program tied to on-time delivery performance, a decision she has said improved retention meaningfully even as it compressed margins in the short term.

## Investing in Route Technology

Bregman has invested steadily in route optimization software and real-time tracking technology, arguing that even a regional logistics company competing on speed needs the same technological sophistication as national carriers, just applied at a smaller geographic scale. Valley Express Logistics developed a proprietary dispatch system in partnership with a local software contractor, allowing the company to dynamically adjust routes throughout the day as new delivery requests come in.

## Looking Beyond the Fox Valley

Valley Express Logistics recently began piloting delivery routes extending toward Fond du Lac and the northern edge of the Milwaukee metro area, a cautious expansion Bregman says will only continue if the company can maintain the delivery speed and reliability that built its reputation in the Fox Valley. "Growing too fast is how you become exactly the kind of slow, generic carrier we started this company to compete against," she said.
